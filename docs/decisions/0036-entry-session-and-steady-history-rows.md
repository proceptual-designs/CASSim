# 0036: One entry session, and history rows that stay put

Status: built 2026-11-25 at Simon's request (review of the maths entry); the browser checks below are on a desktop only. Builds on 0019 (the entry line drawn from controls) and 0035 (one maths tree, shared renderers).

## What was wrong
1. **Three wrappers round one editor.** The Calculator, each Graphs function entry and a spreadsheet cell each wrapped `device/editor.py` in their own way. Graphs used a whole `Calculator` as its edit session, so typing `-x` in a function entry gave `Ans-x`; the spreadsheet had its own copy of insert, template, move and paste, with special cases to dodge the same rule.
2. **A picked history row changed as the cursor moved into it.** The history is LaTeX; a row the cursor is in is drawn from controls so that clicks can place the cursor (0019, run 5 H3). The two drawings differed: full-size fractions against TeX's small ones, wider operator spacing, a result jumping from the right edge to the left, and a cursor bar that took 2 pixels of the row, so everything after it shifted at each step.
3. **One line or two was decided against a fixed width.** An entry and its result shared a line if their character counts fitted 300 handheld pixels, whatever the screen: the wide Computer view put short results underneath.
4. **A long line followed only its end.** The entry line scrolled to its end while the cursor was there and otherwise sat at its start; a long picked row did not scroll at all.

## What is built
- **`device/entry_session.py`: `EntrySession`.** It owns the editor, undo and redo (the same grouping), the clipboard, template specs, and the key actions, with an action table built once. The page's rules are an `EntryRules` (`ans_prefix`, `cells_paste`, `undo`). The Calculator keeps only what is its own (history, recall, Enter, `Ans`); Graphs and the spreadsheet hold a session with no `Ans` rule. The rule itself is now an `Editor` option, off unless asked for.
- **A picked row stays LaTeX.** The first build drew a row the cursor was in from controls, with metrics tuned to the LaTeX and the row held at its LaTeX height. Simon's check the same evening (2026-11-25) showed that cannot work: roots and small fractions were drawn differently, a held row cut its contents off (`ⅇ²`), and a large result (`cSolve(x^3+3x-2=0,x)`) took so many controls that each arrow key was very slow and Flet's `scroll_to` timed out. A picked row is now drawn by the history's own renderer throughout: `Editor.latex()` draws the cursor and the selection, one control a row. The cursor's bar sits between two kerns that take its width back, so it is drawn over the gap and nothing moves as it does; a `+` or `-` that is a sign is put in braces at the cursor, since TeX would otherwise space it as an operator after the bar; an empty slot keeps its box under the cursor. The bar also has to be no taller than the text beside it, or a small fraction, a radical and the brackets round them grow as it passes (Simon's second check): the renderer has no `\smash`, `\rlap` or `\mathrlap` (tried; each is a parser error), so the bar is sized to how small its row is set (`_SMALLER`: 0.64, 0.45 or 0.31 em). Round brackets also went plain whenever the cursor was inside the pair; only a selection's edge inside the pair does that now. Checked in headless Chromium on a nested result with the cursor at 14 places: the formula keeps its size and shape at each; its width differs from the row without a cursor by about 2 pixels.
- **This is a stopgap (Simon, 2026-11-25).** Anything put in the LaTeX takes part in its layout, so a cursor or a selection there can only ever be made nearly neutral. The direction is one renderer of our own: Python lays the maths out from real font metrics and draws it, with the cursor and the selection on a layer above, for the history, the entry line, sheet cells and graph entries alike. Two or more ways of building it are to be tried before one is chosen, and a custom Flutter widget (0035 option C) is expected to replace the drawing later, so the layout and the drawing are to be kept apart.
- **Clicks in a picked row are near, not exact.** The canvas gives no layout, so a tap is matched to the nearest gap at the row's top level from estimated glyph widths (`editor.position_near`, as the first click on a row already was, 0035 stage 3). One, two and three clicks and shift+click work as before. A slot inside a fraction or a root is reached with the arrow keys, not by a click. This gives up the exact click of run 5 H3 a for a row that looks the same and answers at once.
- **The entry line's own look.** It is still drawn from controls (0019), now with TeX's spacing: a medium space round a binary operator, a thick one round a relation, none round a sign or inside an exponent. Its cursor bar takes no width. ⅇ and ⅈ are drawn as the LaTeX draws them, a bold upright e and a bold italic i, not as the double-struck letters of a fallback font (the text keeps ⅇ and ⅈ).
- **One line or two from the width there is.** `Line.width` is the row's drawn width in ems, estimated from its maths tree (`editor.estimated_width`: the canvas gives Python no layout to measure). An entry and its result share a line, entry left and result right, when both and a gap fit the screen as it is drawn now (`body_size`: the Handheld's 320 pixels at its scale, or the Computer view's pane). Otherwise the result goes underneath from the left, as before (run 5, H9 b, d).
- **A long line follows its cursor.** A long entry line or picked row is cut at the edges, with ◂ and ▸ where there is more (the marks keep their room, so the line does not shift as they come and go). It scrolls only when the cursor comes within three ems of an edge (`scroll_offset`), is exactly at its end or start when the cursor is, and its scrolling row is kept between redraws so it keeps its place. A scroll the window does not answer in time is dropped, not raised: the next redraw scrolls again.

## Left open
- The entry line itself still draws full-size fractions while typing; the history echoes them small. Whether the entry line should match needs a look at the emulator.
- The widths are estimates. A line within a few per cent of the screen's width can be judged wrongly (a 6 per cent allowance is added).
- A selection in a picked row is a filled box in the LaTeX, which sets its contents apart: the spacing at its two edges can change by a little as the selection grows.
- **The entry line is still slow on a long entry**, for the reason a picked row was: about ten controls a character are rebuilt and sent on every key (a large result brought down with Enter is the worst case). `#/debug/keytime` prints each redraw's build and send time (`CASSIM: KEY`). The next step is to draw the entry line with far fewer controls, one text run for a stretch of plain characters and one click area a row, or as LaTeX like the history; see 0035 stage 4.
