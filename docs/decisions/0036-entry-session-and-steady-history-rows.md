# 0036: One entry session, and history rows that stay put

Status: built 2026-11-25 at Simon's request (review of the maths entry); the browser checks below are on a desktop only. Builds on 0019 (the entry line drawn from controls) and 0035 (one maths tree, shared renderers).

## What was wrong
1. **Three wrappers round one editor.** The Calculator, each Graphs function entry and a spreadsheet cell each wrapped `device/editor.py` in their own way. Graphs used a whole `Calculator` as its edit session, so typing `-x` in a function entry gave `Ans-x`; the spreadsheet had its own copy of insert, template, move and paste, with special cases to dodge the same rule.
2. **A picked history row changed as the cursor moved into it.** The history is LaTeX; a row the cursor is in is drawn from controls so that clicks can place the cursor (0019, run 5 H3). The two drawings differed: full-size fractions against TeX's small ones, wider operator spacing, a result jumping from the right edge to the left, and a cursor bar that took 2 pixels of the row, so everything after it shifted at each step.
3. **One line or two was decided against a fixed width.** An entry and its result shared a line if their character counts fitted 300 handheld pixels, whatever the screen: the wide Computer view put short results underneath.
4. **A long line followed only its end.** The entry line scrolled to its end while the cursor was there and otherwise sat at its start; a long picked row did not scroll at all.

## What is built
- **`device/entry_session.py`: `EntrySession`.** It owns the editor, undo and redo (the same grouping), the clipboard, template specs, and the key actions, with an action table built once. The page's rules are an `EntryRules` (`ans_prefix`, `cells_paste`, `undo`). The Calculator keeps only what is its own (history, recall, Enter, `Ans`); Graphs and the spreadsheet hold a session with no `Ans` rule. The rule itself is now an `Editor` option, off unless asked for.
- **A row keeps its look.** `EntryLine.inline` draws fractions as TeX sets them in a line of text (0.7 size, close to the bar), and the entry-line renderer now uses TeX's spacing everywhere: a medium space round a binary operator, a thick one round a relation, none round a sign or inside an exponent. A history row shrink-wraps its maths, so a result stays at the right edge. The cursor bar is drawn over the gap and takes no width, and an empty slot keeps its box under the cursor.
- **A row keeps its height.** Flutter's text lines are a little taller than TeX's, so matching metrics still left a row a few pixels taller. Each row drawn as LaTeX reports its height once (rows are kept between redraws, so this is one message a row, not one a key); drawn from controls, the row is held at that height. Checked in Edge on `1/3-1/x`: nothing on the screen moves as the cursor goes in and through it.
- **One line or two from the width there is.** `Line.width` is the row's drawn width in ems, estimated from its maths tree (`editor.estimated_width`: the canvas gives Python no layout to measure). An entry and its result share a line, entry left and result right, when both and a gap fit the screen as it is drawn now (`body_size`: the Handheld's 320 pixels at its scale, or the Computer view's pane). Otherwise the result goes underneath from the left, as before (run 5, H9 b, d).
- **A long line follows its cursor.** A long entry line or picked row is cut at the edges, with ◂ and ▸ where there is more (the marks keep their room, so the line does not shift as they come and go). It scrolls only when the cursor comes within three ems of an edge (`scroll_offset`), is exactly at its end or start when the cursor is, and its scrolling row is kept between redraws so it keeps its place.

## Left open
- The entry line itself still draws full-size fractions while typing; the history echoes them small. Whether the entry line should match needs a look at the emulator.
- The widths are estimates. A line within a few per cent of the screen's width can be judged wrongly (a 6 per cent allowance is added). Measuring a row's real width, as its height now is, would settle it.
- A click on a row that was not picked first is drawn from controls at once, before any height has been reported for a row made in the same redraw; it then takes its natural height.
- Typing speed on a long entry is poor and unmeasured on a Chromebook: about ten controls a character are rebuilt and sent on every key. `#/debug/keytime` shows each redraw's build and send time (status line and a `CASSIM: KEY` console line). See 0035 stage 4 for the options.
