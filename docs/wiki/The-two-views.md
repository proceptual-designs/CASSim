# The two views

## Handheld

Looks like the calculator: a screen with the on-screen keypad below it. Tap or click the keys, or type on a keyboard. On a phone this view fills the screen: the screen and the keys both reach across it.

- Drag the bar between the screen and the keypad to change their sizes.
- The keypad letters can be ABC or QWERTY (see [[Settings]]).
- In full screen (Doc, **Fullscreen**) or the installed app, the calculator's screen grows taller to use the whole height, up to the top edge of the phone.
- In the installed app on a phone, the screen uses the camera cutout area by default. Choose Doc, **Avoid cutout** to keep the calculator within the safe area, or **Use cutout** to use that space again. The choice lasts until the app is closed or reloaded.

## Computer

A larger layout for a laptop or desktop with a hardware keyboard, with a page list and the variables beside the screen. The keypad can be shown beside the screen when you want it.

Click **Pages** or **Variables** to hide or show each section. On a Problem's first page, use its
button to collapse or expand the other pages. Both sections scroll when needed.

Variables with the same dotted prefix, such as `stat.` or `tvm.`, can be collapsed together;
they remain separate variables. Lists and matrices start on one line, and their **…** button
expands them into numbered items or rows. Click the button again to collapse them. Long values show
more items in batches with **Show more**.

On a tablet, tap the entry line (or a spreadsheet cell) and the device's own on-screen keyboard opens, so you can type there too.

## Switching

Choose the view you start in under **Doc, Settings & Status, Handheld Setup** (see [[Settings]]). CASSim remembers it. The first time it opens, a narrow screen starts in Handheld and a wide one in Computer. Doc, **Computer view** (or **Handheld view**) switches at any time. A phone has only Handheld view: there is not enough room for Computer view and the on-screen keyboard, so Doc, **Computer view** is greyed there.
